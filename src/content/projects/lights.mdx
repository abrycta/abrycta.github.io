## Problem

The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) sets official poverty figures for the
country's 1,600+ cities and municipalities through its small-area estimates,
which it releases every few years (2012, 2015, 2018, 2021, 2023). Between
releases, and after the latest one, there is no municipal number. Most earlier satellite-based work for
the country is cross-sectional: one map for one year.

## Approach

- **Features:** yearly statistics per city and municipality from VIIRS night-time
  lights and vegetation indices. NDVI comes from MODIS Terra for every year it
  covers in full, then from NOAA-20 VIIRS, calibrated per municipality onto the
  MODIS series. GitHub Actions computes the statistics from Earth Engine and NASA
  LAADS, and an independent zonal-statistics run cross-checks them.
- **Labels:** the PSA small-area estimates and their published standard errors,
  joined on the official geographic code. Training uses every round since 2006;
  satellite features start in 2012.
- **Model:** gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM). From the last PSA round, the
  model estimates how each place moved within its province, adding the national
  trend and the province's previous figure from the PSA poverty survey (FIES)
  where past rounds show they help. Forward-chaining backtests compare it with
  carrying the last official value forward. It builds on
  [a paper I co-authored](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-23515-2_43), which used a recurrent network (GRU). The Rust core
  reproduces the offline-trained model's predictions exactly (largest difference
  3.7 × 10⁻¹⁴).
- **Intervals:** 50 % and 90 % ranges from split-conformal calibration on held-out
  rounds.
- **In the browser:** the WebAssembly build recomputes a municipality instantly for the
  "what if" sliders, each labelled hypothetical.
- **Uncertainty first:** official estimates show their intervals beside the model
  and its backtest error.
- **Languages:** English and Japanese, with captions first.

Paper: M. C. Clemente, J. Dela Cruz, K. R. Mendoza, K. M. Nudo, et al.,
["Temporal Analysis of Nighttime Lights and Vegetation Indices for Poverty
Forecasting: A Recurrent Neural Network Perspective"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-23515-2_43), in _Machine and
Computing Technologies for Sustainable Development_, Lecture Notes in Networks
and Systems, Springer, 2026, pp. 451–461.

## Architecture

Standard pattern: a hexagonal (ports and adapters) core with materialised read
models, delivered as a static Jamstack site. Customisations: one Rust core has
two driving adapters, a command-line tool and WebAssembly in the browser, so the
same code computes every number.

## Where it stands

Complete at [lights.krtmtthw.dev](https://lights.krtmtthw.dev)
since 2026-10-21: a map of
every city and municipality for 2012–2025, with a detail panel, a chart and a
"what if". Every figure also comes in plain words: "about 1 in 4 people", how it
compares with the province and the country, and whether a change exceeds the
margin of error. A short guide explains how to read the map. The source is
[public on GitHub](https://github.com/abrycta/lgu-lights).

Backtests against PSA's published estimates (n ≈ 1,600 towns per round),
compared with carry-forward:

| Test year | Model error | Carry-forward | 90 % ranges held | Province-level error |
| --------- | ----------- | ------------- | ---------------- | -------------------- |
| 2018      | 6.8 pp      | 8.1 pp        | 90 %             | 6.3 pp               |
| 2021      | 8.1 pp      | 8.0 pp        | 75 %             | 6.2 pp               |
| 2023      | 5.5 pp      | 6.3 pp        | 94 %             | 4.8 pp               |

- **Better on average:** mean error of 6.8 points.
- **Trend breaks:** COVID-19 and PSA's method change broke the falling trend
  the model learned, which shows in 2021.
- **Where the gain comes from:** PSA's own history, as in the paper; without the
  satellite features the error moves by at most 0.03 points.

Night lights come from NASA's Black Marble yearly product: S-NPP through 2025,
then NOAA-20 (VJ146A4). Both satellites cover 2025, so that year
scales NOAA-20 onto the S-NPP series.

PSA changed its method in 2018 and some boundaries have changed since, so years
are not strictly comparable. Highly urbanised cities other than the City of
Manila have no PSA small-area figure and show model estimates; Manila's combines
PSA's 14 district estimates, weighted by 2020 population.
