## Problem

A typhoon's history is split across two datasets. Best tracks give the centre
position and intensity every few hours; reanalysis gives the region's hourly wind
and rain as terabytes of gridded data. Most public tools show one or the other, or
only recent seasons, and none answers "which storms hit my town, and how hard?" for
both the Philippines and Japan.

## Approach

- **Tracks:** [IBTrACS](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/international-best-track-archive)
  for the Western Pacific basin, about 3,000 storms since 1940. A sourced crosswalk
  adds the PAGASA local names (Yolanda, Odette).
- **Fields:** ERA5 hourly reanalysis (about 28 km grid, from 1940), licensed CC BY 4.0.
  Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information.
- **Pipeline:** GitHub Actions jobs cut a storm-following window for each hour and
  pack its wind, gust, pressure and rain into WebP frames per storm: lossy for the
  animation, lossless for exact readouts. The same jobs build per-municipality
  exposure tables.
- **Replay:** a static web app animates the frames on a MapLibre map with the best
  track, and a custom WebGL2 layer draws the field and wind particles. The timeline
  table is the map's text alternative.
- **One contract, four backends:** one OpenAPI spec defines search, compare and exposure queries; Java
  (Spring Boot), Node, Rust and Go each implement it, pass the same contract tests
  and can be swapped in behind the same front end.
- **Intensity:** the replay always shows the official best-track intensity beside the
  field, labelled "reanalysis context" because ERA5 smooths the eyewalls of intense
  typhoons and understates their core winds.

## Architecture

Standard pattern: a static-first single-page app over a contract-first API.
Customisations: a same-origin proxy routes `/api/{java,node,rust,go}/` to
four interchangeable backends sharing one PostGIS database. Scheduled jobs
precompute the heavy data, which the replay reads straight from a Hugging Face
dataset with no backend of its own.

## Where it stands

Complete at [replay.krtmtthw.dev](https://replay.krtmtthw.dev). All 2,058 storms replay with their
ERA5 fields. Each frame records the offset between the ERA5 storm centre and the
best track, and each storm its peak-wind time. Compare lines two storms up at their peaks.

All four backends are live: Java (Spring Boot) and Go on Render's free tier, Node (Hono) and
Rust (workers-rs with Axum) on Cloudflare Workers. The "Under the hood" page in
the app shows them at work.

The pipeline re-fetches the best tracks and rebuilds the storms that changed,
and the live backends are checked against the contract.
