## Problem

The history of a typhoon is split across two very different datasets. Best tracks
give the centre position and intensity every few hours. Reanalysis gives the wind
and rain over the whole region, every hour, but as terabytes of gridded data. Most
public tools show one or the other, or only recent seasons, and none answer
"which storms hit my town, and how hard?" for both the Philippines and Japan.

## Approach

- **Tracks:** [IBTrACS](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/international-best-track-archive)
  v04r01, Western Pacific basin: about 3,000 storms since 1940. PAGASA local names
  (Yolanda, Odette) are not in IBTrACS, so a sourced name crosswalk joins them.
- **Fields:** ERA5 hourly reanalysis (about 28 km grid, from 1940), read from the
  public cloud copies without an account. ERA5 is CC BY 4.0: contains modified
  Copernicus Climate Change Service information.
- **Pipeline:** scheduled GitHub Actions jobs cut a storm-following window for each
  hour and compress wind, gust, pressure and rain into small WebP frames per storm:
  lossy sheets for drawing the hourly animation, and lossless sheets every third hour
  that keep exact values for the readouts. The same jobs build per-municipality
  exposure tables as partitioned Parquet.
- **Replay:** a static web app animates the frames on a MapLibre map with the best
  track, drawing the field and wind particles in a custom WebGL2 layer, in English
  and Japanese. The timeline table is the text alternative to the map.
- **One contract, four backends:** the dynamic parts (search, compare, exposure
  queries) are specified once in OpenAPI and implemented four times, in Java
  (Spring Boot), Node, Rust and Go. Each backend passes the same contract tests and
  can be swapped in behind the same front end.
- **Honesty about the data:** reanalysis smooths the eyewall of intense typhoons, so
  the replay labels the field as "reanalysis context" and shows the best-track
  intensity beside it.

## Architecture

Standard pattern: a static-first single-page app over a contract-first API.
Customisations: one origin and four engines, so a same-origin proxy routes
`/api/{java,node,rust,go}/` to four interchangeable backends that share one
PostGIS database; the heavy data is precomputed by scheduled jobs and read
straight from a Hugging Face dataset, so the replay itself needs no backend.

## Where it stands

Complete at [replay.krtmtthw.dev](https://replay.krtmtthw.dev), in
English and Japanese. All 2,058 storms in the catalogue are replayable with their
ERA5 fields (data version 4, 2026-10-21). Version 4 adds, for every frame, the
offset between the ERA5 storm centre and the best track, and the time of each
storm's peak wind; Compare lines two storms up at their peaks.

All four backends are live, implement the shared contract and pass the same
contract tests: Java (Spring Boot) and Go on Render's free tier, Node (Hono) and
Rust (workers-rs with Axum) on Cloudflare Workers. The "Under the hood" page in
the app shows them at work.

The project is in maintenance, with no new milestones. Each week the pipeline
re-fetches the best tracks and rebuilds the storms that changed, source snapshots
go to a private archive, and the live backends are checked against the contract.
A daily budget check keeps every service within its free tier. When Render's
monthly free hours run low, that check pauses the Go backend until the next month.

Known limitation: ERA5 understates the winds in the cores of intense typhoons,
which is why the field is labelled as reanalysis and the official best-track
intensity is always shown beside it.
